The science · why this app exists

The cycle modulates almost everything.
Generic apps only show almost nothing.

Forty years of literature show that the menstrual cycle systematically affects pain, sleep, cognition, mood, immune response, glucose, thermoregulation, and more. The signals are there. They're individual. They live in your data. Below: the science, the gap, and the engine that closes it.

A 28-day system, but your 28 days

Four phases. Different chemistry in each.

Estradiol peaks around ovulation. Progesterone dominates the luteal phase. Both crash before bleeding starts. Each phase changes pain thresholds, REM sleep duration, working memory performance, insulin sensitivity, and core body temperature.

The "average" 28-day cycle doesn't exist for any single person. Your cycle could be 23 days or 35. Your luteal phase could last 9 or 16. Your ovulation could shift week to week. The map only matters when it's drawn for you.

The statistical engine

Three classical tests. Run on-device. Reported only when significant.

No machine learning hype. No server inference. Just well-understood statistics, executed locally with Apple's Accelerate framework, gated at p < 0.05, and presented in observed-pattern language.

Pearson r

Linear correlation between two continuous signals. CycleSync uses it for biometric-vs-symptom relationships — for example, nocturnal HRV against next-day reported energy.

HRV vs. next-day energyr = +0.61

Chi-squared

Independence test for categorical pairs. Used for "does symptom X occur more often in phase Y than chance?" — for example, migraine occurrence by cycle phase.

Migraine × phaseχ² p < 0.001

Cyclicity test

Determines whether a symptom's distribution across cycle days is significantly non-uniform. Tells you which symptoms are hormonal and which aren't — separating the cyclic from the random.

Why on-device matters

Of every byte the app processes, ~99% never leaves the phone.

The single network exception is the optional Gemma 3 4B model download — model weights only, one-time, opt-in. No cycle log, no symptom, no HealthKit reading, no AI prompt or response is ever sent over the network. The architecture is enforced by a build-time test that fails CI if anyone tries to add a network call outside that one allowed path.

Wellness apps have been subpoenaed. Period data has been sold. The simplest defense is to not have the data on a server in the first place.
